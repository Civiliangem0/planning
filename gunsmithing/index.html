Gunsmithing

Guns are hard to make. Every gun is built from realistic parts made on different machines. How good each part turns out sets the gun's stats and its colour, and guns wear out, jam and overheat like in Tarkov. This replaces the current PERPHeads 5-part recipes for every gun.

  • Bought once, never lost. The press and CNC machine are a big one-time purchase from the dealer, one of each per player. If the owner leaves, the machine stays through the 5-minute disconnect grace, then goes back to their storage with its queue and output. It can't be destroyed or confiscated. The workbench and gun bench switch to the same rule.
  • Access list. The owner adds players or their whole organization. Only they can craft on it. It isn't key-based, so a stolen key gives no control.
  • Raids. Anyone who gets inside can open a machine and take its finished output, plus a blueprint USB left in it. The machine itself can't be carried off.
  • Noise tells players, never police. Nearby players hear the press and CNC, quieter through walls. It never alerts police and isn't grounds for a police raid.

Blueprint USB

The scarce part
AK-47 7/10
Uses left in the corner, rarity strip along the bottom
  • One drive per gun, covering all its pressed and machined parts. It must be in the machine while they're made.
  • 10 uses. One use is one full gun's set of parts, so a drive makes 10 guns. At 0 it's corrupted and gone.
  • Can't be copied. Every drive is unique.
  • The gun bench doesn't need it; assembly only uses the parts.

Where drives come from

  • The dealer's rare, rotating stock, very expensive. Better guns are rarer.
  • Robberies and loot, once those systems exist.
  • Experts scan a real gun on a computer. The gun is taken apart and destroyed, and the scan always succeeds. Higher skill is faster. Computers and blank drives are legal and sold at a tech store.

  • Steel Sheet feeds the press. It's sold at Hammer Plus, cheap and legal.
  • Steel Bar feeds the CNC machine. It's made at the workbench from Refined Metal, so good bar needs high-purity metal. Barrels and bolts are the hard part, as in real life.
  • Better steel and a higher Firearms level raise the range a part's quality can roll in (for example 70–90%). Luck picks the exact number.

These are real gun steels. A 416R stainless barrel is what precision rifles use.

  • Parts, attachments and materials get no colour. A part shows its quality as a number ("AK Barrel · 87%") so it can be judged and traded.
  • A gun's tier is the weighted average of its parts, with the barrel and receiver counting most. Then assembly rolls "fit and finish", which can move it a tier up or down. Contraband is rolled here.
  • The colour is locked at assembly, like a CS2 skin. Swapping parts later changes the stats, never the colour.
  • Big gap: a top-tier gun is about 30%+ better than Mil-Spec.
  • Contraband: best stats and a unique look, such as an engraving or a different finish.
  • Guns nobody crafted (police issue, older guns) are Mil-Spec.
  • Blueprints are tiered by how rare the gun is.

Example numbers. Actual wear and repair loss are tuned in playtesting.

  • The maximum comes from the parts. Every shot wears the gun a little, and a hot gun wears faster.
  • Repair at a gun bench with a Gun Repair Kit (made at the workbench). The gun stays in the bench for a while. Each repair lowers the maximum, and a higher Firearms level loses less.
  • Gunsmiths can repair other players' guns for money. Worn-out guns are scrapped for Scrap Metal and their undamaged parts.
  • Police guns wear too, but reset at the armory for free.

Jams & heat

In the fight
  • Jams are almost never a problem on a healthy gun. They get much more likely as durability drops, and more again with a poor bolt, a poor magazine or a hot barrel.
  • A jammed gun clicks and won't fire. Press R to rack it, which takes about a second.
  • Magazines come off the press with a quality number. Bad ones cause feed jams.
  • Overheating: long bursts heat the gun. A hot gun is less accurate, jams more and wears faster, then cools down. Better barrels handle more heat.

Police & market

Where guns go
  • Police seize illegal items (blueprints, guns, gun parts, drugs). They're destroyed and pay out by value, more for rarer and better ones. Machines and legal materials are never seized.
  • The dealer buys guns, priced by colour, stats and condition. It never sells guns, so crafted and looted guns are the only supply.

A crafter's path

Draft · to discuss
  1. Workbench: Refined Metal, Steel Bar, repair kits. Sell to other crafters.
  2. Buy a machine and make legal materials to pay it back.
  3. Level Firearms: pistol parts first, rifles later.
  4. Get a blueprint and start making gun parts. From here it's illegal.
  5. Gun bench: assemble, reveal colours, repair for money.
  6. Expert: scan guns into new blueprints and sell them.

Build order

Each step built and playtested before the next
  1. Storage keeps used items whole, like Tarkov's stash, so a stored gun keeps its wear, attachments and magazine. Today storage only keeps counts, so a worn gun would come back as new.
  2. Gun durability: current and maximum, wear per shot, shown on inspect.
  3. Repair at the gun bench, and scrapping.
  4. Jams (R clears) and overheating.
  5. Benches stay through the disconnect grace, then go to storage.
  6. Part quality and rarity: rolls, gun tier, the colour strip, stats from parts.
  7. Steel: Sheet at Hammer Plus, Bar at the workbench, in three grades.
  8. Blueprint USBs and the dealer's rare stock.
  9. CNC machine, then the stamping press.
  10. New part lists for every gun. The 26 old PERPHeads parts are removed, and the AK-47 is added (it needs a model first).
  11. Expert blueprint scanning on a computer, plus the tech store.
  12. Police seizing with the payout. Confiscation doesn't exist yet.
  13. Later: blueprints from robberies, the police armory, corrupt cops.
